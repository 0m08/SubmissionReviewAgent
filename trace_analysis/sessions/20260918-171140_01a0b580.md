Title: Holding and Push-Out
Content: After the pipe is fully inserted, do not let go right away. Solvent cement joints can "push out," where the pipe starts to slide back out of the fitting before the bond has set. To prevent this, hold the pipe and fitting together firmly. The manufacturer's instructions will tell you exactly how long to hold, but it is typically between 15 and 30 seconds. If you are working in cold weather, you will need to hold the joint longer because the cement sets more slowly.

Title: Handling vs. Curing
Content: After assembly, wipe off any excess cement from the outside of the joint with a clean rag; a small, even bead should remain around the entire joint. There is a big difference between handling strength and cure time. Handling strength is the time you wait—often about 15 minutes—before you can move the assembly without breaking the bond. Cure time is the much longer period required for the joint to reach full strength. Always follow the manufacturer's chart for cure times based on your specific pipe size, temperature, and humidity.

Title: Solvent Cement Joint Summary
Content: Solvent welding chemically fuses parts into one solid piece. Success depends on identifying the material correctly—PVC often needs primer, but ABS never does. Check your cement's shelf life and throw out anything lumpy or over three years old. Pay attention to the weather; extreme heat or cold changes set times, and wet pipe needs specialized cement. Always follow manufacturer instructions for application sequence, rotation, and hold times. Finally, respect the difference between handling strength and full cure time, and never use compressed air for testing.
